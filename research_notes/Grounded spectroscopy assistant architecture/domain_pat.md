## Q1. How do PAT / spectroscopy feasibility studies work in practice (method selection, sampling interface, calibration and reference methods, validation, transfer, go/no-go), and which guidance applies?

### Takeaway
There is no single "feasibility study standard". The practical workflow is assembled from the FDA PAT framework (2004), the FDA NIR guidance (2021), ICH Q14 (analytical target profile, multivariate model lifecycle) and ICH Q2(R2) (two-phase multivariate validation with an independent validation set), plus the EMA NIR guideline and ASTM E1655/D6122 for process analyzers. All of them converge on the same logic: define the performance need first, build a calibration set that spans every real source of variability, measure the reference method well, validate on independent batches, then plan model maintenance. A go/no-go rule is not defined in any source I found. It must be built from the target performance criteria versus demonstrated RMSEP, reference-method error and robustness evidence.

### Cited Findings

#### FDA PAT framework (2004) [REG]
- FDA's PAT guidance defines three measurement modes: at-line (sample removed, isolated and analysed close to the process stream), on-line (sample diverted from the process, may be returned), in-line (sample not removed from the process stream; can be invasive or noninvasive). — [FDA PAT guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/71012/download)
- The four PAT tool categories listed are multivariate tools for design/data acquisition/analysis, process analyzers, process control tools, and continuous improvement/knowledge management. — [FDA PAT guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/71012/download)
- Process-analyzer measurements "need not be absolute values of the attribute of interest"; relative differences (within a lot, lot-to-lot, supplier-to-supplier) can be enough for process control. — [FDA PAT guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/71012/download)
- "Design and construction of the process equipment, the analyzer, and their interfaces are critical to ensure that collected data are relevant and representative of process and product attributes." Installation on existing equipment should follow a risk analysis so it does not adversely affect process or product quality. — [FDA PAT guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/71012/download)
- Multivariate methods are "often necessary" for real-time control; a simple correlation may need a mechanistic justification depending on risk. — [FDA PAT guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/71012/download)
- The guidance points to ASTM practices for process analyzers (e.g. D3764, Practice for Validation of Process Stream Analyzer Systems, is in its references). — [FDA PAT guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/71012/download)

#### FDA NIR guidance (August 2021) [REG]
- Off-line/at-line versus on-line/in-line: on-line/in-line normally needs a custom-built interface to get acceptable signal-to-noise and acquisition time. Example interfaces: a sapphire window built into a blender wall, or a fibre-coupled probe for drying. The guidance adds that "an NIR signal transmitted through a fiber optic cable should be adequate for the intended measurement". — [FDA NIR guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/91343/download)
- Interface location (position, distance, depth) must give spectra that represent the properties of the entire processed material; interface consistency and cleanliness must be maintained throughout acquisition. — [FDA NIR guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/91343/download)
- Transmission versus reflectance: transmission probes the bulk but typically has a more limited useful spectral range and more noise; reflectance includes a surface component but usually gives lower noise and wider useful range. — [FDA NIR guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/91343/download)
- Sampling plan should ensure samples represent the processed material, rest on rational criteria and include time- and location-based sampling. — [FDA NIR guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/91343/download)
- Calibration set should (1) cover an appropriate concentration range, (2) address sources of variability (process, analyzer, physical characteristics, water content, temperature) and (3) cover expected process-parameter variation; commercial-scale or representative batches are ideal. — [FDA NIR guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/91343/download)
- Use an equivalent interface and sample presentation for calibration and routine spectra. — [FDA NIR guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/91343/download)
- Number of latent variables should be chosen with statistical tools; "Establishing the number of factors based on the number of components in the analyte sample is not considered to be a suitable substitute"; overfitting lowers robustness. — [FDA NIR guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/91343/download)
- NIR procedures need periodic evaluation because they are highly sensitive to process and incoming-material changes and offer "only limited availability of real-time reference standards". — [FDA NIR guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/91343/download)

#### ICH Q14 (adopted 2023) and ICH Q2(R2) [REG]
- Both guidelines were adopted by the ICH Assembly after its 31 October 2023 meeting (Prague); Q2(R2) extends validation principles to spectroscopic/multivariate data (NIR, Raman, NMR, MS). — [GMP Publishing summary [STD]](https://www.gmp-publishing.com/content/en/gmp-news/news-about-gmp-cgmp/d/ich-parallel-guidances-q2-r2-q14-on-analytical-procedures-adopted)
- Q14 Analytical Target Profile (ATP): a description of intended purpose, and performance characteristics with associated criteria for one or more quality attributes; "The ATP drives the choice of technology" and is later the basis for validation (Q2) and lifecycle management. Formal submission of an ATP is optional. — [ICH Q14](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_Q14_Guideline_2023_1116.pdf)
- Q14 chapter 8 (multivariate procedures): development includes scientifically justified sample selection and distribution over the range, sample size, variable selection and data pre-processing. Q14 explicitly covers latent-variable models; "machine learning (e.g., neural networks)" is acknowledged as "could use similar principles" but "will not be discussed in detail". — [ICH Q14](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_Q14_Guideline_2023_1116.pdf)
- Q14: uncertainty of the reference analytical procedure must be "sufficiently low in relation to the intended performance" of the multivariate procedure; prepared reference samples must be homogeneous. — [ICH Q14](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_Q14_Guideline_2023_1116.pdf)
- Q14: sample population should encompass sources of variability (raw material quality, process variability, storage conditions, sample preparation, testing). Lab and pilot-scale samples are often used for variability; commercial-scale samples are recommended to capture equipment-specific effects. Number of samples depends on matrix complexity ("for more complex sample matrices generally more samples are needed"). A validation set from independent batches can demonstrate robustness. — [ICH Q14](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_Q14_Guideline_2023_1116.pdf)
- Q14: too many latent variables cause overfitting, lower robustness and need for more frequent model updates; justify the final number. — [ICH Q14](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_Q14_Guideline_2023_1116.pdf)
- Q14 model maintenance: diagnostics include x-residuals/F-probability and outlier statistics (Hotelling's T-squared, Mahalanobis distance); periodic and event-driven confirmatory testing against reference results; a model maintenance plan is the last step of model establishment. Unacceptable performance can be a measurement-system problem (Q14's example: "a misaligned sample interface") with no model update needed. — [ICH Q14](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_Q14_Guideline_2023_1116.pdf)
- Q14 Annex B gives model-lifecycle examples by impact category: on-line NIR blending in development = low impact; NIR content uniformity/assay for release = high impact; Raman identity test on incoming raw material = high impact (needs ATP, formal risk assessment, DoE over raw material, lots, packaging, instrument-to-instrument, user, software limits, and a suitable alternative procedure if the Raman procedure fails). — [ICH Q14 Annex B](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_Q14_Guideline_2023_1116.pdf)
- Q14 section 10.3 lists what to document for a multivariate model used for specifications/RTRT: validation set with independent samples, performance criteria, evaluation of results vs criteria, relationship between model performance and specification limits, and an overview of monitoring/maintenance (outlier diagnostics). Also the measurement principle, sample presentation, interrogation time, measurement frequency, how calibration data were obtained, reference procedure and model type. — [ICH Q14](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_Q14_Guideline_2023_1116.pdf)
- Q2(R2) section 2.5: multivariate validation has two phases: model development (calibration + internal testing, e.g. choosing the number of PLS latent variables) and model validation with a validation set of independent samples. For identification libraries, validate with challenge samples not represented in the library. — [ICH Q2(R2)](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_Q2%28R2%29_Guideline_2023_1130.pdf)
- Q2(R2): the reference analytical procedure's performance "should equal or exceed the expected performance of the multivariate analytical procedure"; reference analysis and spectral collection should be on the same samples within a reasonable time to ensure sample/measurement stability. — [ICH Q2(R2)](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_Q2%28R2%29_Guideline_2023_1130.pdf)
- Q2(R2): accuracy of a multivariate procedure depends on calibration-sample distribution across the range and reference-method error; use RMSEP; if RMSEP is comparable to RMSEC the model is sufficiently accurate on an independent set; for classification use misclassification rate or positive prediction rate; plot residuals across the working range; "the routine metrics of RMSEP encompass accuracy and precision". — [ICH Q2(R2)](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_Q2%28R2%29_Guideline_2023_1130.pdf)
- Q2(R2) Table 10 (NIR tablet assay example): specificity via loading plots and outlier rejection; repeatability with sample removed from holder between measurements; accuracy "using an appropriate number of determinations and concentration levels (e.g., 5 concentrations, 3 replicates)"; robustness factors include drug-substance/excipient sources, water content, tablet hardness, orientation in holder. — [ICH Q2(R2)](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_Q2%28R2%29_Guideline_2023_1130.pdf)

#### EMA NIR guideline (EMEA/CHMP/CVMP/QWP/17760/2009 Rev1) [REG]
- Lists measurement variables to control: environment, glassware optical quality, cleanliness of the sample interface, optical path length, sample optical properties/temperature/thickness, flow, polymorphism, particle size, residual moisture and solvents, homogeneity, sample age. — [EMA NIR guideline](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/guideline-use-near-infrared-spectroscopy-pharmaceutical-industry-and-data-requirements-new-submissions-and-variations_en.pdf)
- Calibration/validation samples should span analyte concentration, particle size, suppliers, water, residual solvents, matrix changes, process variation over extended periods, sample age and temperature; validation set must be "completely independent" of the calibration set. — [EMA NIR guideline](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/guideline-use-near-infrared-spectroscopy-pharmaceutical-industry-and-data-requirements-new-submissions-and-variations_en.pdf)
- Robustness should cover temperature, humidity, sample position in the optical window, presentation devices and "probe depth"; DoE may be used. — [EMA NIR guideline](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/guideline-use-near-infrared-spectroscopy-pharmaceutical-industry-and-data-requirements-new-submissions-and-variations_en.pdf)
- Report SEC (calibration), SECV (cross validation) and SEP (validation); "The calibration range should be at least 10 x SEP". It also states the number of calibration samples depends on matrix complexity and the applicant must justify composition and number of samples. A calibration test set is not independent validation. — [EMA NIR guideline](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/guideline-use-near-infrared-spectroscopy-pharmaceutical-industry-and-data-requirements-new-submissions-and-variations_en.pdf)
- A draft Revision 2 of this guideline exists (I did not read it). — [EMA draft Rev 2](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/draft-guideline-use-near-infrared-spectroscopy-pharmaceutical-industry-and-data-requirements-new-submissions-and-variations-revision-2_en.pdf) [SNIPPET]

#### ASTM E1655, E2617, D6122 [STD, secondary]
- E1655 covers multivariate calibration of infrared spectrometers from near-IR (about 780-2500 nm) through mid-IR; current listing shown as E1655-17 (revision date reported as Nov 1, 2024). It addresses validity at calibration time; ongoing validation of predictions on unknown samples is covered separately (e.g. D6122). — [Nimonik E1655 listing](https://www.standards.nimonik.com/products/astm/e1655/) [SNIPPET]
- A process-NMR vendor page summarising E1655/D6122 practice states: at least six reference measurements per sample (E1655), check reference repeatability on at least three calibration samples spanning the range, outliers include high-leverage samples (E1655 sec. 15), validation per E1655 sec. 18 tests for bias and agreement; online validation follows D6122 sec. 12; periodic validation uses control charts of analyzer-minus-lab differences; Dixon's test for replicate reference outliers. — [process-nmr.com](https://process-nmr.com/initial-calibration-and-validation/) [VENDOR, secondary on the standards]
- A US patent attributes to E1655 the rule "number of samples used in developing the calibration must be at least six (6) times the number of variables", with a separate validation set; I could not confirm this from the standard itself. — [US 8,322,200](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8322200) (unverified attribution)
- A 2010 forum post claims E1655 requires at least 20 validation samples and a t-test at 95%, and that ASTM E2617 specifies stricter validation conditions. Unverified user recollection. — [IM Publications forum](https://www.impublications.com/discus/messages/5/8405.html?1291323850)

#### Practice examples of calibration design and validation [PEER / TRADE, older]
- A bioreactor NIR study validated with "several complete batches ... not part of the calibration set" (5 calibration and 3 validation batches); analytes were highly correlated (cell growth dominates), and the glutamine model "was not able to predict the spiking", so "Models based on correlations are not recommended for process control"; spiking experiments break correlations. — [PMC3980391 NIR bioprocess review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3980391/) (publication year not confirmed; likely about 2014)
- Calibration transfer is a recognised separate workstream: instrument-matching and standardization approaches (DS, PDS, OSC, variable selection); transfer needs standardization samples measured on both instruments; newer methods (Mobius/affine, TEAM) claim lower transfer error on NIR datasets. — [Spectroscopy Online transfer series](https://www.spectroscopyonline.com/view/transfer-chemometrics-part-i-review-subject-0); [arXiv 1701.09143](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.09143); [Quimica Nova review](https://doaj.org/article/5bf6e58fbf5345c5a1c4a64fcc5ae1f4) [SNIPPET]

### Inferences
- A defensible feasibility-study skeleton, synthesised from the above (not a published standard): (1) write an ATP-style requirement (analyte, range, required accuracy as RMSEP-or-equivalent, measurement frequency, process conditions); (2) risk assessment of variability sources and interface risks (temperature, fouling, flow, bubbles, particle size, windows); (3) pick technique + probe geometry; (4) bench or pilot data collection on representative samples with all variability sources deliberately spanned; (5) reference analytics with error low relative to the target (replicates, SEL); (6) exploratory analysis then PLS/classification with internal testing; (7) independent-batch validation (RMSEP vs RMSEC, bias, residual plots, range/SEP); (8) robustness runs (temperature, probe position/depth, fouling, instrument/fibre swap); (9) model-maintenance and transfer plan; (10) go/no-go against the ATP-style criteria.
- Go/no-go criteria should be expressed as comparisons the assistant can compute from user-supplied numbers (target accuracy vs reference-method error vs demonstrated RMSEP; range ≥ 10×SEP as an EMA-style check; RMSEP≈RMSEC; coverage of variability sources), never as invented thresholds.
- The assistant should distinguish "applicant for regulated release use" (Q2/Q14/EMA/FDA NIR/USP/EP relevant, high-impact category) from "process development/monitoring use" (lower-impact categories in Q14 Annex B), because the evidence burden differs greatly. A client enquiry that does not state the GMP context should trigger a clarification question about it.
- Cross-technique, the same regulatory skeleton (independent validation, reference error, variability coverage, maintenance) applies to Raman and MIR even though the EMA/FDA documents are NIR-specific; Q2(R2)/Q14 are technique-agnostic.
- Because the Q14 lifecycle ends in a model maintenance plan, a feasibility report should include a "what we'd monitor in routine use" section (outlier statistics, confirmatory testing triggers).

## Q2. Which decision factors and "traps" do experts check (water in MIR/NIR, fluorescence in Raman, fibre transmission and cable length, sampling representativeness, fouling, temperature, particle size)?

### Takeaway
Experts decide on technique by (a) matrix (aqueous vs non-aqueous, solid vs liquid, turbid), (b) analyte band strength/selectivity, (c) probe/fibre material limits, and (d) calibration burden. The recurring traps are water absorption (NIR/MIR) versus fluorescence and weak signal (Raman), fibre/crystal spectral cut-offs and bend/temperature limits, non-representative interfaces, window fouling, temperature-induced band shifts, scatter from particles/cells/bubbles, and chance correlations between analytes. The most useful assistant behaviour is turning each trap into a targeted clarification question and a risk-register entry with a mitigation experiment.

### Cited Findings

#### Technique selection
- Both NIR and MIR are strongly affected by water absorption, limiting use in aqueous systems such as fermentations; Raman is advantageous for aqueous analysis; NIR needs no sample prep/dilution at high concentration. — [Applied Sciences 2019 fermentation study via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/4160f06c7f114de2a4a79dc633ba860a) [SNIPPET]; [Bocklitz, Schmitt, Popp, Spectroscopy 2020](https://www.spectroscopyonline.com/view/optical-molecular-spectroscopy-combination-artificial-intelligence-process-analytical-technology) [TRADE]
- Bocklitz et al.: NIR is less selective and sensitive than MIR; Raman can suffer from fluorescence background and weak signals; the main PAT challenge named is "the still missing automation of process analytical techniques". — [Spectroscopy 2020](https://www.spectroscopyonline.com/view/optical-molecular-spectroscopy-combination-artificial-intelligence-process-analytical-technology) [TRADE]
- NIR vs mid-IR selection article (Spectroscopy, 2006 per the search snippet; author not captured): NIR water is "reasonably transparent" and cheap glass/quartz optics and fibres are usable; MIR needs "extremely short effective pathlengths" around 10-100 um, its fibres "tend to be very expensive and quite difficult to manipulate"; MIR transmitting materials can be less chemically resistant; MIR ATR "works best in following reactions in liquid process streams"; NIR bands are weak, overlapping overtones, so quantitation is complex and NIR calibrations "are not portable between instruments", whereas MIR calibration is "much more generic"; MIR better for identification and functional-group-resolved mixtures; "neither region is universally better". — [Spectroscopy Online](https://www.spectroscopyonline.com/view/nir-versus-mid-ir-how-choose) [TRADE, dated]
- Pharma adoption: NIR still predominant, Raman increasingly widespread. A patent example on tablet coating notes Raman signals were available for seven of nine coating runs (signal-availability risk). — [US 9,539,176](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9539176) [SNIPPET]

#### Raman fluorescence
- Raman intensity scales with the fourth power of excitation frequency; longer excitation (785/1064 nm) reduces fluorescence but weakens Raman. Photobleaching can quench fluorescence but may harm repeatability and "deteriorate the performance of calibration models". SERDS (two excitation wavelengths, difference spectrum) needs a tunable/dual laser and is noise-sensitive; time gating needs pulsed lasers/fast detectors and works when fluorescence is delayed relative to Raman; computational baseline correction can introduce artifacts and cannot recover SNR. — [Timegate Instruments blog [VENDOR, sells time-gated Raman]](https://www.timegate.com/blog/time-gating-and-other-fluorescence-suppression-techniques)
- A handheld-Raman vendor states 785 nm is often called the "sweet spot" yet roughly 10% of Raman-active materials fluoresce under 785 nm excitation. — [Metrohm blog [VENDOR]](https://metrohm.com/bg_bg/discover/blog/20-21/the-evolution-of-handheld-785-nm-raman-spectroscopy--raman-extra.html) [SNIPPET]
- A 2021 Analyst paper applied SERDS (784/786 nm) to strongly autofluorescent samples and found it superior to several baseline-correction algorithms. — [Analyst 2021](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlepdf/2021/an/d1an01376a) [PEER, SNIPPET]; a 2025 review on time-gated Raman exists. — [Laser & Optoelectronics Progress 2025](https://m.researching.cn/articles/OJfa7ed6b2fcf2797) [SNIPPET]

- Silica UV-Vis-NIR cables: "0.18-1.2 um (UV-VIS) or 0.35-2.4 um (VIS-NIR)", "ultra low OH content", lengths "up to 350 m", NA 0.22, core 100-1500 um; attenuation not stated on the page. — [Art Photonics silica cables](https://artphotonics.com/product/silica-uv-vis-nir-cables/)

#### Sampling representativeness, fouling, temperature, particle size
- Theory of Sampling: representativity is a property of the sampling process, not of an individual sample; heterogeneity splits into distributional and compositional; sampling uncertainty can be estimated by replication experiments or variography. — [Esbensen via LBMA Alchemist](https://www.lbma.org.uk/alchemist/issue-85/sampling-theory-and-practice); [The Analytical Scientist](https://theanalyticalscientist.com/techniques-tools/why-we-need-the-theory-of-sampling)
- Regulators: interface position must give spectra representing the entire process material (FDA NIR guidance, above); EMA lists "cleanliness of the sample interface" and "probe depth" among variables; Q14's example of a measurement-system cause is "a misaligned sample interface". — [FDA NIR](https://www.fda.gov/media/91343/download); [EMA NIR](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/guideline-use-near-infrared-spectroscopy-pharmaceutical-industry-and-data-requirements-new-submissions-and-variations_en.pdf); [ICH Q14](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_Q14_Guideline_2023_1116.pdf)
- Fouling: a Raman probe patent notes process streams "tend to coat the optic and stop data collection", and criticises ultrasonic cleaning (poor in viscous fluids), spray jets (large solvent volumes) and mechanical wipers (sealing risk); GEA markets a probe with in-process cleaning and window-contamination self-check. — [US 6,873,409](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6873409); [GEA Lighthouse Probe [VENDOR]](https://www.gea.com/pl/products/automation-control/analytical-in-line-control-measurement/lighthouse-probe)
- Temperature: it causes non-linear band shift and broadening in mixtures and a model built at fixed temperatures predicts accurately only at those temperatures; particle size/shape, sample packing, cell growth produce multiplicative scatter that can mask chemical signal. — [ESCAPE-18 paper](https://skoge.folk.ntnu.no/prost/proceedings/escape18/Topic_5/Oral/FP_00290.doc) [SNIPPET]
- Bioprocess NIR: poor transferability between cell-free and cell-containing media is attributed to band overlap, scattering and added cell absorbance. — [Pharmaceutical Manufacturing](https://www.pharmamanufacturing.com/articles/2008/172/) [SNIPPET, 2008, trade press]
- Chance correlations: "correlations found by software can occur by chance", hence chemometric models must be validated on an independent sample set. — [EMA NIR guideline](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/guideline-use-near-infrared-spectroscopy-pharmaceutical-industry-and-data-requirements-new-submissions-and-variations_en.pdf)
- ATR penetration: depth grows with wavelength and falls with incidence angle and crystal index; dp is not a transmission pathlength ("a peak with a dp of 1 micron will not be equivalent to a transmission spectrum peak" at 1 micron). — [Specac](https://www.specac.com/en/news/resource/depth-of-penetration-for-atr-measurements); [Shimadzu FAQ](https://www.ssi.shimadzu.com/service-support/faq/ftir/2/index.html)

### Inferences
- Trap-to-question map the assistant can use: water content/aqueous? (NIR/MIR water bands vs Raman); fluorescent matrix, colour, additives, and what excitation wavelengths are acceptable? (Raman fluorescence); cable length to the instrument, pipe/vessel geometry, temperature and pressure at the probe? (fibre loss, bend radius, tip limits); flow regime, solids, bubbles, viscosity, fouling tendency, cleaning in place? (representativeness and window fouling); temperature range and swing? (band shift, calibration range); particle size distribution and variability? (scatter); is the target analyte correlated with others in the process? (spurious correlation); GMP/release use? (Q14 impact tier).
- Vendor fibre figures are single-wavelength-band typicals (e.g. loss quoted at 2.5-5 um excluding 4.0-4.5 um in one statement), and the two statements on the same page disagree (0.2-0.3 vs 0.2-0.4 dB/m). The assistant should treat them as vendor typicals with an "unverified for your configuration" flag and ask for the datasheet or a spectral loss curve before any budget is presented as a promise.
- Silica vs chalcogenide vs polycrystalline can be framed as a spectral-coverage-and-cost-and-robustness decision: silica for UV-Vis-NIR and Raman (cheap, long runs, high temperature); chalcogenide for roughly 1.1-6.5 um (limited temperature, tighter bend); silver halide for 3-17 um fingerprint region (chemical/mechanical handling and no stated toxicity but bend limits). Specific band-coverage statements need to come from vendor data tables, not model memory.
- Most "traps" are measurable in a feasibility study, so each one maps to an experiment (spiked samples, temperature ramp, fouling test, probe-position test); this is how the plan should present risks.

### Gaps
- No quantitative, peer-reviewed comparison table of Raman vs NIR vs MIR with decision criteria for in-line use (post-2020) found; the technique-selection sources are trade articles, a 2019 fermentation paper and vendor pages.
- Vendor-to-vendor differences in usable fibre windows: one search summary quoted a process-spectroscopy source giving chalcogenide about 2 to 6-10 um and silver-halide 4-17 um, which differs from the Art Photonics pages (1.1-6.5 and 3-17 um); I could not recover that source's URL, so it is not cited. The assistant should always use the specific fibre datasheet.
- Fibre attenuation curves versus wavelength (including OH, S-H, Se-H absorption bands for chalcogenide) and practical maximum lengths for chalcogenide/PIR were not found on the vendor pages; need datasheets (Art Photonics TN documents, MSDS, handling manual are linked but not read).
- No Raman-specific or MIR ATR-specific fouling literature found beyond one patent and one vendor.
- Temperature-effect references were old or snippet-level; no quantitative figures obtained.

## Q3. Useful deterministic domain tools the assistant can expose

### Takeaway
Everything that can be computed should be: unit/shift conversions, fibre loss budgets from datasheet inputs, ATR penetration depth from refractive indices, spectral-window coverage checks (probe + fibre + detector + analyte band), model-statistic checks (RMSEP, SEP, range/SEP, bias, Hotelling T-squared flags), and a method-selection matrix populated only from sourced attributes. Sample-size outputs must be framed as planning prompts with cited rules, not recommended sample counts, because the authoritative standards do not specify universal numbers.

### Cited Findings
- Existing calculators confirm the conversion workflow is routine: Raman shift calculators take excitation wavelength (nm) and shift (nm or cm-1) and give the scattered wavelength. — [photonetc Raman shift calculator](https://photonetc.com/raman-shift-calculator) [VENDOR/utility]
- ATR penetration depth is defined as the distance at which the evanescent-wave intensity amplitude falls to 1/e; variables are wavelength, incidence angle, IRE index n1 and sample index n2. Table values at 45 degrees and n2 = 1.5: diamond/ZnSe 2.01 um at 1000 cm-1 and 1.00 um at 2000 cm-1; Si 0.85/0.42 um; Ge 0.66/0.33 um. Effective pathlength = effective depth x number of bounces; dp is for comparing peak intensities across a spectrum, not an absolute transmission pathlength; polarisation assumptions apply. — [Specac](https://www.specac.com/en/news/resource/depth-of-penetration-for-atr-measurements)
- The commonly stated formula is dp = lambda / (2 pi n1 sqrt(sin^2(theta) - (n2/n1)^2)); one source uses the wavelength inside the prism (lambda0/n1), so convention must be stated. Anton Paar's example gives about 2.0 um for ZnSe, sample n = 1.5, 10 um. — [Shimadzu FAQ](https://www.ssi.shimadzu.com/service-support/faq/ftir/2/index.html); [Anton Paar wiki](https://wiki.anton-paar.com/cz-cs/zeslabeny-uplny-odraz-atr/) [SNIPPET]
- MIR transmission pathlengths are typically 10-100 um. — [Spectroscopy Online](https://www.spectroscopyonline.com/view/nir-versus-mid-ir-how-choose)
- Fibre data inputs available from the manufacturer pages: attenuation (dB/m) in stated windows, Fresnel loss, spectral range, core diameters, bend radii, temperature/pressure (see Q2). — [Art Photonics](https://artphotonics.com/product/chalcogenide-mid-ir-fibers/); [PIR](https://artphotonics.com/product/polycrystalline-infrared-fibers/)
- Validation/statistics rules to implement as calculators: EMA "calibration range should be at least 10 x SEP", SEP reported without bias correction, SEC/SECV/SEP triplet; ICH Q2(R2) RMSEP vs RMSEC comparison, residual vs reference plot, example design 5 levels x 3 replicates; ICH Q14 outlier statistics Hotelling's T-squared, Mahalanobis distance, x-residuals; E1655 practice of >= 6 reference replicates per sample and Dixon's test for replicate outliers (via D3764 as summarised by a vendor). — [EMA](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/guideline-use-near-infrared-spectroscopy-pharmaceutical-industry-and-data-requirements-new-submissions-and-variations_en.pdf); [ICH Q2(R2)](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_Q2%28R2%29_Guideline_2023_1130.pdf); [ICH Q14](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_Q14_Guideline_2023_1116.pdf); [process-nmr.com](https://process-nmr.com/initial-calibration-and-validation/)
- Raman intensity ~ (excitation frequency)^4 gives the sensitivity-vs-fluorescence trade-off. — [Timegate](https://www.timegate.com/blog/time-gating-and-other-fluorescence-suppression-techniques)
- Open-source chemometric building blocks exist (Python `chemometrics` package: PLS calibration with preprocessing, EMSC, Whittaker filters, MCR in notebooks). — [chemometrics docs](https://chemometrics.readthedocs.io/en/stable/examples.html) [SNIPPET]

### Inferences
- Verified/computed reference values the tool layer can be unit-tested against (computed by me, standard physics, vacuum wavelengths, no dispersion correction): wavenumber (cm-1) = 10^7 / wavelength (nm). Excitation 532 nm = 18,797.0 cm-1; 785 nm = 12,738.9 cm-1; 1064 nm = 9,398.5 cm-1. Raman scattered line = 10^7 / (10^7/lambda_exc - shift): for a 1000 cm-1 shift, 532 nm gives 561.9 nm, 785 nm gives 851.9 nm, 1064 nm gives 1190.7 nm; for 3000 cm-1, 532 nm gives 633.0 nm, 785 nm gives 1026.8 nm, 1064 nm gives 1562.9 nm. Practical implication the tool should flag: at 785 nm a 3000 cm-1 (C-H/O-H stretch) shift lands near 1027 nm, beyond the useful range of many silicon CCD detectors, and at 1064 nm needs an InGaAs-type detector; detector response is a detector-datasheet input, not a model assumption.
- The ATR depth formula with n1 = 2.4, 3.4, 4.0 (diamond/ZnSe, Si, Ge), n2 = 1.5, theta = 45 degrees reproduces the Specac table exactly (2.01/1.00, 0.85/0.42, 0.66/0.33 um at 1000/2000 cm-1) (computed by me), which gives a ready unit test. The tool should require explicit n1, n2, angle, and flag when n2/n1 approaches sin(theta) (critical-angle regime, formula breaks down), and when the sample index is unknown ask rather than guess.
- Fibre budget arithmetic (computed): transmitted fraction = 10^(-(alpha*L + connector/coupling losses)/10); Fresnel loss for a high-index fibre end face: 1-(1-R)^2 with R = ((n-1)/(n+1))^2. Using n = 2.4 gives 31%, which matches the vendor's 31% for chalcogenide; an assumed n of about 2.2 (my assumption, not from a source) gives about 26%, in the neighbourhood of the 25% quoted for PIR. This lets the tool cross-check vendor numbers. Example (using vendor 0.3 dB/m only as an illustrative input): 10 m = 3.0 dB = 50% transmitted before Fresnel and connector losses; the tool must label such outputs "illustrative, datasheet-dependent, not a guarantee" and require the wavelength-specific attenuation as input.
- A "spectral window feasibility" tool: intersect (fibre transmission window) AND (ATR crystal window) AND (detector range) AND (analyte band positions from a sourced library) AND (water/solvent absorption bands) and return pass/partial/fail with the limiting component. Analyte band positions are not in my sourced material: they should come from a curated, cited table (not from LLM memory).
- A method-selection matrix tool should output a structured table (rows: Raman, NIR, MIR-ATR, UV-Vis, fluorescence; columns: aqueous compatibility, fibre cost/length, probe types, calibration burden, fluorescence risk, typical risks) where every cell carries a citation ID or is marked "assistant judgement". Sourced seeds are in Q2.
- Validation-set planning tool: must restrict itself to (a) listing the cited rules (independent batches, variability coverage, reference replicates, 5x3 design example, range >= 10 x SEP, RMSEP vs RMSEC), (b) computing what a user's proposed plan implies (e.g. number of independent batches, covered ranges, whether range/SEP >= 10 given a target SEP), and (c) warning if reference-method error is not demonstrably below target. It should not output a "recommended N" without a cited basis.
- Also implement as pure functions: RMSEP/SEP/bias/slope from paired reference-predicted lists; leverage/Hotelling T-squared/Q-residual check for user-supplied PLS outputs; Dixon's test; calibration range / SEP ratio; PLS latent-variable plausibility flag vs sample count (heuristic, labelled).

### Gaps
- No authoritative published formula for chalcogenide attenuation spectrum, bending loss or coupling loss; only vendor typicals.
- No sourced library of analyte band positions or water/solvent windows gathered here (needs a separate curated spectra-reference source such as NIST, SDBS or vendor tables).
- Sample-size heuristics: the "6 x variables" rule and "20 validation samples" claim are unverified. Do not hardcode them as rules.
- ATR dp formula source pages gave images or snippets, so exact convention (lambda vs lambda/n1) is confirmed only via my numerical reproduction of Specac's table.

## Q4. Existing LLM / AI tools for spectroscopy, chemometrics and PAT (2023-2026): what they do and their limits

### Takeaway
The literature is dominated by structure elucidation (IR/NMR/MS to SMILES) and benchmarks, not quantitative process spectroscopy. Only one agent paper (LUMIR) targets quantitative NIR-type regression, and its results are self-reported on a preprint landing page. Benchmarks show general LLMs lack spectral grounding (they reason well but do not read spectra reliably). Vendor offerings in PAT are mostly classical chemometrics with automation (Infometrix Ai-Metrix, Metrohm Vision Air, Eigenvector); I found no evidence of a vendor LLM "PAT copilot". Regulators (ICH Q14) treat ML only briefly. Hence a feasibility assistant has an open niche but cannot lean on a validated precedent.

### Cited Findings

#### LLM agents and benchmarks
- LUMIR (arXiv 2507.21471, July 2025, v2 Aug 2025): agent framework for infrared spectral analysis under low-data conditions that uses an LLM to mine a structured literature knowledge base for validated preprocessing/feature strategies, then few-shot prompting for classification, regression and anomaly detection; datasets include milk NIR, Chinese herbs, CRP storage, industrial wastewater COD, Tecator and Corn; abstract claims "comparable to or surpassing" ML/DL baselines "especially in resource-limited settings" with no numbers on the landing page. — [arXiv 2507.21471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21471) [PREPRINT]
- IR-Agent (arXiv 2508.16112, ICLR 2026): multi-agent structure elucidation from IR: transformer translator + table-interpretation expert + retrieval expert + elucidation expert. On 9,052 NIST experimental spectra the Top-1 exact match was 0.103 for IR-Agent (o3-mini) versus 0.098 for a transformer baseline (within reported noise); Top-10 0.216 vs 0.176 (0.278 with atom-type hints). Limitations: peak positions only (no shapes/intensities), needs retraining per dataset, performance degrades with too many candidates, hallucination risk; authors recommend expert supervision; no quantitative or process analysis. — [IR-Agent HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.16112v1) [PREPRINT/PEER, ICLR 2026]
- SpectraLLM (ICLR 2026): Qwen3-32B backbone with LoRA fine-tuning; converts characteristic peaks from IR, Raman, UV, NMR, MS into text and generates SMILES; IR+Raman was the strongest pairing. — [ICLR 2026 abstract](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/2aad9aaf0e14591f7b7d4770752eec89-Abstract-Conference.html) [PEER, SNIPPET]
- SpecX benchmark (arXiv 2605.18791, May 2026; NeurIPS 2026 submission): 1.7M molecules; eight modalities including IR, Raman, UV, fluorescence. Findings: specialised models excel at signal-level tasks; multimodal LLMs "exhibit strengths in high-level reasoning but lack precise spectral grounding"; on functional-group inference DeepSeek-V3 and GPT-4.1-mini reached F1 0.379-0.408 on the small subset, "substantially below 1D-CNN baselines"; a 3B model collapsed to F1 0.000; authors judge LLM performance on quantitative tasks "highly unsatisfactory for practical applications"; experimental subset limited to UV-Vis and MS, mostly simulated data (sim-to-real gap); they suggest tool-use plugins as future work. — [SpecX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18791) (read from PDF) [PREPRINT]
- SpectrumWorld/SpectrumBench (arXiv 2508.01188) is another multimodal spectroscopy benchmark including Raman images; results not read. — [arXiv 2508.01188](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.01188v4) [PREPRINT, SNIPPET]
- "LLMs as Lab Engineers" (Findings of ACL 2026): benchmark for analytical method lifecycle management (LC-MS/GC-MS instrument readiness, system suitability, method development, troubleshooting). Findings: general-purpose commercial models often beat chemistry-specialised ones; retrieval augmentation and human-guided chain-of-thought both help; six-dimension rubric: specificity, correctness, usefulness, feasibility, misinformation risk, error handling. — [ACL Anthology preview](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-acl/2026.findings-acl.2015/) [PEER, abstract only]
- ChemBench (Mirza et al., Nature Chemistry 2025): ~2,700 chemistry questions; top models on average outperformed the best human chemists in that study. — [arXiv 2404.01475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01475v1) [PEER/PREPRINT, SNIPPET]; note this is general chemistry knowledge, not instrument or process-spectroscopy reasoning.

#### Spectroscopy foundation models and generative models
- Astronomy has pretrained spectral foundation models (SpectraFM ~90k synthetic stellar spectra; OmniSpectra ~5.5M spectra across eight surveys; SpecCLIP). They are not relevant to vibrational/PAT spectra. — [SpectraFM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.04750); [OmniSpectra](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.15351); [SpecCLIP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01939) [PREPRINT, SNIPPET]
- Vibrational: Vib2Mol (Raman+IR spectrum to structure, claims better than prior work on experimental data), RamanGPT (crystals, GNN + fine-tuned LLM, qualitative experimental check on one material), Mimyria (ML surrogates for IR/Raman of aqueous systems), and an IR foundation model reported at 100M+ parameters pretrained on ~60M simulated spectra. A survey (SpectraML roadmap, Guo et al.) says the field moves toward few/zero-shot learning and large-scale pretraining. — [Vib2Mol](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07014v1); [RamanGPT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03764); [Mimyria](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.06760); [survey 2502.09897](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2502.09897); [IR foundation model, RSC Digital Discovery 2025 (one of the two RSC PDFs in the search results; which is not confirmed)](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlepdf/2025/dd/d5dd00131e) [PREPRINT/PEER, SNIPPET]
- None of these target process quantification, probe design or feasibility scoping. The Spectroscopy Online review of 2025 generative-AI works (GANs, VAEs, GNNs, a transformer for structure generation) records data scarcity and instrument variability as key limits and "does not mention hallucination". — [Workman, Spectroscopy Online, 16 Feb 2026](https://www.spectroscopyonline.com/view/generative-artificial-intelligence-in-spectroscopy-what-s-new-and-a-glossary-of-terms) [TRADE]

#### Perspective on AI vs chemometrics
- Workman (19 Feb 2026): AI is "an extension of established multivariate methods rather than a replacement"; adds nonlinear modelling, feature learning, synthetic spectra for augmenting limited calibration sets, fusion with process data, post hoc explanation; risks: overfitting ("greater risk of unintentional errors"), explanations that reveal spurious correlations (baseline artifacts mistaken for chemical signal); independent test sets still needed. Perspective piece, no benchmarks; does not discuss LLMs. — [Spectroscopy Online](https://www.spectroscopyonline.com/view/artificial-intelligence-as-the-next-layer-of-chemometrics) [TRADE]
- Workman (9 Feb 2026) roundup of 2023-2025 Raman biopharma papers: automated robotic calibration/data collection (e.g. quality measurements every 38 s; about 45 s per sample; CQA prediction at 28 s intervals in Protein A chromatography), deep learning claimed to address baseline drift, fluorescence and low signal; does not discuss calibration-model transfer; the article's own claim that AI is "essential" for regulatory acceptability is the author's framing, not a regulatory finding. — [Spectroscopy Online](https://www.spectroscopyonline.com/view/top-10-most-influential-articles-on-raman-spectroscopy-in-biopharmaceutical-applications-during-2023-2025) [TRADE]
- A 2025 review on ML for small data in upstream bioprocessing notes Raman needs a separate model to turn spectra into measurements and that bioprocessing data are scarce. — [arXiv 2506.12322](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.12322) [PREPRINT, SNIPPET]

#### Vendor and commercial tools
- Infometrix Ai-Metrix: cloud service to automate calibration of NIR/IR/Raman analyzers; features an objective calibration-sample selection, automated guidance on number of factors, automated detection that a calibration needs updating; vendor-neutral; vendor claims it performed consistently with experienced chemometricians (2018 conference) and about 100x faster at 20% of cost. Unverified marketing; ATC 2026 presentation planned. — [Infometrix Ai-Metrix](https://infometrix.com/aimetrix/); [brochure](https://infometrix.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/ai-metrix-general-brochure.pdf); [ATC 2026](https://infometrix.com/tag/ai-metrix/) [VENDOR]
- Metrohm Vision Air: routine Vis-NIR software reporting parameters/trends rather than spectra; models from Eigenvector PLS_Toolbox/Solo can be run via integrated Solo_Predictor. — [Technology Networks](https://www.technologynetworks.com/analysis/product-news/metrohm-releases-vision-air-220007); [Eigenvector-Metrohm partnership](https://eigenvector.com/eigenvector-research-inc-and-metrohm-announce-global-partnership/) [VENDOR, SNIPPET]
- Bruker OPUS described by a third-party directory as classical chemometrics (PLS, PCA, library search), not generative AI. — [KI-Syndikat directory](https://www.ki-syndikat.de/tools/bruker-opus/) [SNIPPET, secondary]
- Trade article (Hydrocarbon Processing, Feb 2025) argues AI can replace chemometric modelling for spectrometric analyzers; only the preview was accessible. — [Hydrocarbon Processing](https://hydrocarbonprocessing.com/magazine/2025/february-2025/special-focus-digital-technologies/use-ai-to-replace-spectrometric-analyzer-chemometric-modeling/) [TRADE, SNIPPET, low confidence]
- No Thermo Fisher OMNIC Paradigm AI feature, no Endress+Hauser/Kaiser Raman Rxn LLM feature, no Sartorius/Cytiva PAT copilot found in searches (search results did not address them). — search results; absence of evidence only.

#### Regulatory stance on AI/ML
- ICH Q14 says latent-variable models are in scope, while machine-learning (e.g. neural networks) "will not be discussed in detail". — [ICH Q14](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_Q14_Guideline_2023_1116.pdf)

### Inferences
- Evidence that LLM-only spectral reading is unreliable is strong and recent (SpecX; IR-Agent's modest gains and own hallucination warning). The architecture should keep the LLM as a planner/explainer that calls deterministic tools and a retrieval layer over cited documents, and should never ask it to read or "describe" spectral data from images or tables unless a signal-level tool produced the numbers.
- The "LLMs as Lab Engineers" finding (retrieval and reasoning help; general models beat specialised ones) supports using a general frontier model with RAG over standards, vendor datasheets and application notes rather than a fine-tuned chemistry model. The rubric (specificity, correctness, usefulness, feasibility, misinformation risk, error handling) is a ready evaluation scheme for the assistant's outputs.
- LUMIR-style "literature knowledge base to select preprocessing" is the nearest precedent for the chemometrics-planning part, but evidence is self-reported on a preprint landing page; treat as an idea, not a validated method.
- Ai-Metrix indicates that automation of calibration-sample selection and factor-count guidance exists commercially; the assistant need not replace chemometric modelling, only scope and plan it (and optionally interoperate with existing chemometric software).
- This looks like an under-served niche (pre-sales feasibility scoping tied to fibre/probe hardware), but absence of evidence in my searches is not proof of absence of commercial products; claims of novelty should be hedged.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed evaluation of an LLM assistant on PAT feasibility scoping, probe selection or quantitative chemometrics; no study quantifying hallucination of spectral values, detection limits or accuracies in this setting.
- No verified vendor LLM copilots for PAT (Thermo, Bruker, Metrohm, Endress+Hauser, Sartorius, Cytiva, Siemens SIPAT, CAMO, Prediktera, Eigenvector) found; vendor press pages were not searched directly.
- Full texts of LUMIR, SpectraLLM, SpectrumWorld and the RSC IR foundation-model paper not read; reported performance numbers for those are unconfirmed.
- Raman-specific benchmark results for general LLMs unconfirmed; SpecX Raman results not extracted in detail.

## Q5. What do scientists value in a report UI (traceability, comparison tables, spectral-window charts, uncertainty statements)?

### Takeaway
I found no usability study of spectroscopists or PAT scientists' report preferences. The best available evidence is indirect: what regulators ask to be documented and plotted (traceable sources, reference method, calibration/validation sets, residual and predicted-vs-reference plots, outlier diagnostics), data-integrity expectations (attributable, original, audit trail), and the LLM-evaluation literature that flags misinformation risk and the need for expert oversight. The UI design recommendations below are therefore inferences from regulatory documentation norms, not measured user preferences.

### Cited Findings
- Q14 section 10.3 lists the items a multivariate procedure dossier should show: independent validation set, performance criteria, results versus criteria, relation of model performance to specification limits, monitoring/outlier approach, measurement principle and instrument parameters, how calibration data were obtained, model type. — [ICH Q14](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_Q14_Guideline_2023_1116.pdf)
- Q2(R2) and EMA expect plots: residuals across the working range, predicted vs reference, and reported SEC/SECV/SEP; EMA wants calibration and validation sets compared and differences discussed; outliers handled with reported statistical parameters. — [ICH Q2(R2)](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_Q2%28R2%29_Guideline_2023_1130.pdf); [EMA NIR guideline](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/guideline-use-near-infrared-spectroscopy-pharmaceutical-industry-and-data-requirements-new-submissions-and-variations_en.pdf)
- FDA submission expectations for NIR include the sampling accessories, acquisition times, number of spectra and scans, replicates, wavelength ranges, batch counts and sizes, number of samples from each batch in the calibration/validation sets, and data showing probe-window location suitability. — [FDA NIR guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/91343/download)
- Data integrity: FDA's data-integrity Q&A defines ALCOA as attributable, legible, contemporaneously recorded, original or true copy, and accurate, and defines an audit trail as a secure, computer-generated, time-stamped record. — [FDA data integrity guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/119267/download)
- Ph. Eur. 2.2.48 revision explicitly draws attention to access to spectra, instrument settings and measurement parameters. — [Metrohm summary [VENDOR]](https://metrohm.com/en_us/discover/blog/20-21/ph--eur--2-2-48-raman-spectroscopy--how-raman-instruments-from-m.html)
- IR-Agent authors recommend expert supervision because of hallucination risk; Workman cautions that post hoc explanations may reveal spurious correlations and that independent test sets remain necessary. — [IR-Agent](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.16112v1); [Workman 2026](https://www.spectroscopyonline.com/view/artificial-intelligence-as-the-next-layer-of-chemometrics)
- "LLMs as Lab Engineers" uses a misinformation-risk and error-handling dimension when scoring LLM answers, which formalises what an expert reviewer checks. — [ACL Anthology preview](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-acl/2026.findings-acl.2015/)
- ASTM D6122 practice (as summarised) uses control charts of analyzer-minus-lab differences for periodic validation, a familiar visual format for process-analyzer users. — [process-nmr.com](https://process-nmr.com/initial-calibration-and-validation/)

### Inferences
- Traceability: every number, range, or recommendation should carry a provenance label that distinguishes (a) user-provided input, (b) quoted from a cited document/datasheet (with link and date), (c) computed by a named deterministic tool (with inputs and formula version), and (d) assistant judgement/assumption.
