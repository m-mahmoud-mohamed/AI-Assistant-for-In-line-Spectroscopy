# PRISM: presentation video script (max. 5:00)

The form asks to: demonstrate the prototype on a customer scenario, show its clarification questions,
recommendations, supporting sources and feasibility-study plan, and explain the architecture and main limitations.

**Before recording**
- Start the app (`docker compose up --build`, or `make api` + `make web`) with `PRISM_LLM_PROFILE=openai` in `.env`.
- Open http://localhost:5173 in a clean browser window at 1440×900; light theme.
- Keep `slides/PRISM_slides.pdf` open in a second window.
- Copy the demo enquiry (the H04 scenario from `materials/customer_scenarios_for_final_evaluation.txt`) to the clipboard.
- Do one dry run first: a run takes ~25 s, a follow-up ~10–30 s. Cut waiting time in editing if needed.

| Time | Screen | What to say |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Slide 1 | "This is PRISM, an AI assistant that turns a customer's in-line spectroscopy enquiry into a grounded first technical assessment for Art Fiber Systems." |
| 0:20–0:55 | Slide 2 | "A plain language model invents specifications, fills in missing client facts and quotes lab studies as proof. PRISM is built the other way round: a deterministic rules engine decides the status and which methods are possible, the model only writes and explains, every AFS claim cites a verbatim quote from the supplied materials, and the assistant asks before it guesses." |
| 0:55–1:35 | Slides 3 and 4 | "The enquiry is read into facts with quotes. Fourteen rules over the AFS method table decide candidates and contradictions. A clarify gate asks only the questions whose answers change the recommendation. Three writers draft the sections from retrieved evidence cards, and every sentence is verified: citations must exist, quotes must be verbatim, no invented numbers, and a judge checks each claim against its source. The AFS materials are the knowledge base: page store, the method table as a graph, and 71 curated evidence cards." |
| 1:35–2:05 | App: paste the H04 enquiry, press Enter | "Here is a customer: water content in a petroleum line with MIR ATR, an analyzer in another building, and they want to reuse a silica fiber from a NIR setup." Show the progress line, then the verdict preview and the question card: "Within seconds the engine gives a preliminary verdict and asks what changes the recommendation. Each option shows its effect. I don't know the optical state yet, so I answer 'Not known yet'." Click Apply. |
| 2:05–2:50 | Report pane: Overview | "Status: insufficient information, not approved. The premise check says the silica fiber reuse is contradicted: silica transmits up to about 2.4 µm, mid-infrared starts at 2.5 µm." Click a citation chip: "Every claim opens its source page in the AFS material, highlighted." |
| 2:50–3:20 | Methods, Spectral, Concept tabs | "The recommended concept: MIR ATR, conditionally, with a mid-infrared fiber such as polycrystalline silver halide or a hollow waveguide, and the spectral range." |
| 3:20–3:45 | Plan and Questions tabs | "A first feasibility study in six steps, with reference measurements and a go/no-go decision against agreed criteria, and the ranked questions for the client." |
| 3:45–4:15 | Chat: type "The cable run to the analyzer would be about 80 m." then ask "Convert 2.9 µm to wavenumbers." | "New facts revise only the affected sections and create report version 2; 'What changed' shows the difference. Questions are answered from the sources and the user's data without changing the report." Show Export → PDF briefly. |
| 4:15–4:40 | Slides 8 and 9 | "All six final scenarios were generated by PRISM without editing; a replay check reproduces them byte for byte. On the development cases the decision status matched in 7 of 8, with zero invented numbers and every citation and quote verified." |
| 4:40–5:00 | Slide 10 | "Limits: the knowledge base covers 71 curated cards so far, about half of the judged claims are only partly supported by their quote and are marked as such, and reading the enquiry can still misplace a fact. Next: expert review of more cards and measured spectra from the AFS lab. Thank you." |

**Tips**
- Speak while the run is going; the progress line shows the real steps.
- If a live call is slow, cut the wait in editing; never edit the shown output.
- Keep the mouse still while you talk about a section; zoom the recording to the report pane for the citation click.
