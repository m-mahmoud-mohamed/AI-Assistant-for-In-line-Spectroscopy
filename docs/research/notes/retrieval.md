# Retrieval and knowledge-representation strategies for a grounded expert assistant over a tiny curated corpus (2024-2026)

Scope note: the target system answers in-line process-spectroscopy enquiries from about 60 pages (vendor slides, application notes, papers, one comparison table), curated into about 70-200 evidence cards (claim, verbatim quote, page, scope label) plus a small typed graph. Models are open-weight behind an OpenAI-compatible API at 30 requests/min (Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, DeepSeek V4 Flash, Gemma 4 31B, Qwen3-Embedding-4B). The current system is graph-first selection plus BM25 plus embeddings, fused with reciprocal rank fusion (RRF).

Evidence-quality legend used below: [peer-reviewed] = venue confirmed on the fetched page or search result; [preprint] = arXiv only, venue not verified here; [vendor] = company blog or technical report with self-reported numbers; [secondary] = blog or aggregator, or taken from a search snippet; [inference-from-sources] appears only under Inferences. Almost every benchmark below used corpora far larger than 60 pages and models smaller or older than the Qwen3.5-class models you will use, so effect sizes transfer only loosely.

---

## 1. Vector RAG vs hybrid (BM25 + dense) + cross-encoder reranker: measured gains, reranker latency

### Takeaway
Hybrid retrieval and a reranker are the best-documented, cheapest upgrades over dense-only RAG at scale (Anthropic reports retrieval-failure rates falling from 5.7% to 1.9% with contextual embeddings, contextual BM25 and reranking). Their benefit is a ranking-precision effect that shrinks when the whole candidate pool is only 70-200 short cards, and a 4B-class reranker adds roughly a second of latency per query, so a reranker is a later-stage option for this corpus, not a first-stage need.

### Cited Findings
- Adding BM25 to a dense retriever (LLM-Embedder) raised nDCG@10 from 70.20 to 72.50 on TREC DL19 and from 68.76 to 69.80 on DL20, with recall@1k up from 84.48 to 89.08 on DL19; latency rose from 2.61 to 3.20 (DL19) and 0.71 to 0.77 (DL20) per query. The paper weights the sparse score at alpha = 0.3. General web-search corpora, not technical QA; preprint (venue not verified here). — [Wang et al., Searching for Best Practices in RAG (2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01219)
- In the same study a reranker changed end-to-end RAG score from 0.530 (no rerank) to 0.580 (monoT5), 0.551 (monoBERT), 0.558 (RankLLaMA) and 0.536 (TILDEv2); total pipeline latency per query went from 10.31 s to 11.71 s (monoT5), 11.65 s (monoBERT), 13.51 s (RankLLaMA), 11.26 s (TILDEv2). Standalone reranker latency on MS MARCO dev was 4.5 s (monoT5), 15.8 s (monoBERT), 82.4 s (RankLLaMA), 0.02 s (TILDEv2) per query (candidate depth and hardware as in the paper; not directly comparable to Qwen3 rerankers). The authors' "balanced" recommendation was hybrid retrieval plus a light reranker (TILDEv2). — [Wang et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01219)
- Anthropic's Contextual Retrieval post reports, at top-20 retrieved chunks, a baseline retrieval-failure rate of 5.7%, falling to 3.7% (-35%) with contextual embeddings, 2.9% (-49%) with contextual embeddings plus contextual BM25, and 1.9% (-67%) when a reranker is added (reranking took the top 150 candidates down to 20). Datasets were codebases, fiction, arXiv papers and science papers; top-20 beat top-5 and top-10. Vendor-reported, no peer review. — [Anthropic, Introducing Contextual Retrieval (Sept 2024)](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)
- Qwen3-Embedding-4B scores 69.45 (mean task) on MTEB multilingual and 74.60 on MTEB English v2, against 64.33/70.70 for the 0.6B and 70.58/75.22 for the 8B. Authors' own evaluation (vendor-style technical report, Apache 2.0 release). — [Qwen3 Embedding technical report (June 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05176)
- Reranking nDCG on the top-100 candidates from Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B: Qwen3-Reranker-4B scores MTEB-R 69.76, MMTEB-R 72.74, MLDR 69.97, MTEB-Code 81.20; BGE-reranker-v2-m3 scores 57.03, 58.36, 59.51 and 41.38 respectively; Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B scores 65.80, 66.36, 67.28, 73.42; the 8B is roughly equal to the 4B (69.02 MTEB-R). All are the Qwen authors' own runs, and the table does not include a no-rerank baseline, so it shows reranker-vs-reranker rather than the absolute gain over first-stage retrieval. — [Qwen3 Embedding technical report](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05176)
- Reranker latency data points (cross-setup, hardware not controlled): one 2026 long-context reranking paper reports Qwen3-Reranker-4B P50 about 1,221 ms for 50 query-chunk pairs batched in one pass (about 1,895 ms scored one at a time; about 13.9 GB peak memory); a separate benchmark reports about 1,875 ms for Qwen3-Reranker-8B over 20 candidates on a B200 with small batch size; a separate comparison reports about 241 ms mean per query for bge-reranker-v2-m3 (candidate count and GPU unclear). These come from a search-result summary, not from reading the papers in full. — [arXiv 2602.12192](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.12192) and [arXiv 2605.05726](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.05726), as summarized in a [secondary search result](https://presenc.ai/research/best-open-weight-reranker-models-2026)
- ViDoRe V3 (10 enterprise corpora, about 26,000 pages) found that adding a text reranker (zerank-2) to a Jina-v4 text pipeline lifted nDCG@10 from 50.4 to 63.6 (+13.2), the highest retrieval result in the study, whereas a visual reranker added only +0.2. Shows how large reranking gains can be on hard, large, multi-page enterprise corpora. [peer-reviewed per ACL 2026 listing] — [ViDoRe V3 (Jan 2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08620v1)

### Inferences
- [inference-from-sources] At 70-200 cards, first-stage recall at top-20 is almost certainly near ceiling already (20 of 200 is 10% of the pool), so the Anthropic-style 67% failure reduction is an upper bound that applies mainly when the pool is 1,000+ units. The measurable value of a reranker here is ordering (putting the best two or three cards at the top for the answer prompt), and that can be tested cheaply against the current RRF baseline.
- [inference-from-sources] Latency: a self-hosted bge-reranker-v2-m3 (about 0.1-0.25 s per query) is plausible as a cheap precision stage; Qwen3-Reranker-4B has stronger published scores (especially on code and long documents) but about 1 s per query. Both are small next to answer generation from a 397B MoE model. Whether a reranker call is counted against the 30 requests/min cap depends on whether it is served through the same API; this is unknown.
- [inference-from-sources] RRF over BM25 plus dense is already the "hybrid" configuration that the sources recommend; the incremental step is a reranker, and the evidence for its value on a pool this small is indirect.

### Gaps
- No measured numbers found for hybrid-vs-dense or reranker gains on a corpus of 100-200 units, or specifically on in-line spectroscopy or analytical-chemistry text. The only way to know is an in-house eval with gold card IDs per enquiry.
- No controlled latency comparison of Qwen3-Reranker-4B vs bge-reranker-v2-m3 on one GPU; the numbers above come from different papers and hardware (and from search summaries).
- Unknown whether the deployment offers a /rerank endpoint, and whether reranker calls count toward the 30 requests/min limit.

## 2. GraphRAG, LightRAG, HippoRAG 2, KG-RAG / ontology-grounded RAG vs a hand-built typed graph + rules

### Takeaway
LLM-built graph RAG systems help on multi-hop and corpus-wide "sense-making" questions, not on fact lookup, and they cost 10-100x more to index than plain RAG (GraphRAG: 115.5M input tokens on an 11,656-passage corpus). HippoRAG 2 is the only variant with consistent small gains over a strong dense baseline (avg F1 59.8 vs 57.0). For a tiny, expert-curated corpus, no head-to-head study exists, but the evidence on LLM-extracted graph coverage and on graphs without attached source text favors keeping the hand-built typed graph and attaching verbatim evidence to it.

### Cited Findings
- Microsoft GraphRAG targets query-focused summarization over whole corpora ("What are the main themes in the dataset?") and was evaluated on corpora of about 1M tokens; it builds an LLM-extracted entity graph, pregenerates community summaries and combines partial answers per community. The authors claim improved comprehensiveness and diversity versus a conventional RAG baseline (abstract gives no numbers). Peer-review status not verified. — [Edge et al., GraphRAG (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16130)
- LightRAG adds a graph to text indexing with dual-level (low- and high-level) retrieval and incremental updates; the abstract claims improved retrieval accuracy and efficiency without numbers. — [LightRAG (Oct 2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05779)
- HippoRAG 2 (personalized PageRank over an LLM-built graph plus deeper passage integration) reports average F1 across NQ, PopQA, MuSiQue, 2Wiki, HotpotQA, LV-Eval and NarrativeQA of 59.8, versus 57.0 for the dense NV-Embed-v2 baseline, 53.1 for HippoRAG, 49.6 for GraphRAG, 48.8 for RAPTOR and 6.6 for LightRAG (Llama-3.3-70B reader). The LightRAG figure is implausibly low and looks like a reproduction problem, so treat it as unreliable. — [HippoRAG 2 (Feb 2025), Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14802v2)
- Offline indexing on the MuSiQue corpus (11,656 passages), input/output tokens and time: RAPTOR 1.7M/0.2M, 100.5 min; GraphRAG 115.5M/36.1M, 277.0 min; LightRAG 68.5M/18.3M, 235.0 min; HippoRAG 2 9.2M/3.0M, 99.5 min. The page did not give LLM call counts. — [HippoRAG 2, Table 12](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14802v2)
- Systematic RAG vs GraphRAG comparison (Llama 3.1-8B): RAG wins on single-hop NQ (F1 64.78 vs 63.01 for the best graph variant); HippoRAG 2 wins on multi-hop HotpotQA (63.01 vs RAG 60.04) and MultiHop-RAG (accuracy 70.27 vs 67.02); on NovelQA RAG ties RaptorRAG (57.12) and beats Community-Local (53.03) and Community-Global (39.17). KG-triplets-only retrieval is poor (F1 34.28 NQ, 25.02 HotpotQA). — [Han et al., RAG vs. GraphRAG (Feb 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11371v3)
- Same study, failure modes and cost: only 65.8% of answer entities appear in the LLM-extracted HotpotQA graph (65.5% for NQ); on "null" queries (no answer in corpus) Community-Global answers correctly 19.27% of the time versus 96.01% for plain RAG; on MultiHop-RAG construction took 135 s for RAG, 5,560 s for Community-GraphRAG and 7,702 s for KG-GraphRAG. LLM-as-judge comparisons showed position bias that sometimes reversed verdicts. — [Han et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11371v3)
- GraphRAG-Bench ("When to use graphs in RAG", GPT-4o-mini, novel and medical corpora): fact retrieval is a tie or small edge to vanilla RAG with rerank (novel: RAG 60.92 vs best graph 60.14; medical: 64.73 vs 66.28 HippoRAG 2); complex reasoning favors graphs (novel 42.93 vs 53.38; medical 58.64 vs 61.98, both HippoRAG 2); summarization is mixed; graph methods are more faithful but cover less evidence. Prompt tokens per query: vanilla RAG about 879-954; HippoRAG 2 about 1,008-1,020; RAPTOR about 3,441; MS-GraphRAG local about 38,700; LightRAG about 100,000; MS-GraphRAG global about 331,000. Authors' guideline: use basic RAG for simple lookups, graphs for multi-concept reasoning. — [GraphRAG-Bench (June 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05690)
- Ontology-guided KG RAG on one grant application (20 questions, so very small): vector RAG 12/20 correct, GraphRAG 18/20, ontology KG plus text chunks 18/20, but ontology KG without chunks only 3-4/20; authors say text chunks attached to the graph consistently improved accuracy and that DB-derived ontologies matched text-derived ones at lower cost. Single document, 20 questions; preprint. — [Ontology learning and KG construction vs RAG performance (Nov 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.05991v1)

### Inferences
- [inference-from-sources] Your query mix (what technique fits this process/analyte/fiber; compare methods; what are the constraints) is mostly entity-scoped lookups and two-hop joins (application -> technique -> fiber), which a typed graph with rules handles directly; the benefit that GraphRAG-style systems show (multi-hop, sense-making) is what your hand-built graph and scope labels already provide, with 100% entity coverage by construction versus about 65% for LLM extraction in the Han et al. study.
- [inference-from-sources] Indexing cost estimate, labeled as an extrapolation: GraphRAG consumed roughly 10k input and 3k output tokens per passage on MuSiQue, so even a 200-unit corpus is on the order of 2M input tokens. That is cheap in dollars for open-weight models but is a few hundred to a few thousand LLM calls against a 30 requests/min cap (minutes to hours), plus an extraction-quality audit, and it brings no evidence of better accuracy at this size. The null-query result (19% vs 96% correct abstention) is an additional risk for a system that must say "not in the corpus".
- [inference-from-sources] The strongest supported hybrid is: hand-built typed graph for scoping and expansion, with evidence cards (verbatim quote and page) attached to nodes, since graph-without-text collapsed to 15-20% in the ontology study. This is what you already have. HippoRAG 2 is the only LLM-graph method worth a later trial (compact prompts, gains on multi-hop) if multi-hop evals fail.

### Gaps
- No study found that compares an expert-curated typed graph with rules against GraphRAG / LightRAG / HippoRAG 2 on a corpus of under 1,000 units. A paper on expert-curated ground-truth graphs (arXiv 2609.17291, 126 curated passages) surfaced in search but only excerpts were seen; not used for claims.
- GraphRAG-Bench indexing-cost appendix was not readable in the fetched text; GraphRAG's own paper win rates were not visible in the abstract page.
- All graph-RAG benchmarks used general-domain corpora (Wikipedia QA, novels, medical text) and 8B-70B readers, not Qwen3.5-class models.

## 3. RAPTOR and contextual retrieval (contextual embeddings + contextual BM25)

### Takeaway
Contextual retrieval has the best-documented effect size and is a natural fit (prepend situating context to each unit before embedding and BM25), but the headline numbers come from chunked long documents; your cards already carry their own claim, page, and scope, so the equivalent is a cheap deterministic header. RAPTOR (recursive summary tree) targets long-narrative multi-hop questions and is not a good match for atomic technical claims.

### Cited Findings
- Contextual Retrieval: prepending LLM-generated chunk context before embedding and BM25 cut top-20 retrieval failures by 35% (embeddings alone), 49% (embeddings plus BM25) and 67% (plus reranking), relative to a 5.7% baseline; one-time cost reported as $1.02 per million document tokens (800-token chunks, 8k-token documents, 100 tokens of context per chunk, using prompt caching). Vendor claim. — [Anthropic (Sept 2024)](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)
- Same post states that if the knowledge base is under 200,000 tokens (about 500 pages) it can simply be placed in the prompt, and that prompt caching reduces latency by more than 2x and cost by up to 90%. Vendor claim, measured on Claude models. — [Anthropic (Sept 2024)](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)
- RAPTOR builds a tree by recursively embedding, clustering and summarizing chunks, retrieving across levels of abstraction; with GPT-4 it reports a 20% absolute accuracy improvement on QuALITY (long-document multiple-choice QA). The abstract does not discuss cost. — [Sarthi et al., RAPTOR (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.18059)
- RAPTOR-style retrieval is cheap to build relative to the graph methods (1.7M input / 0.2M output tokens, 100.5 min on the 11,656-passage MuSiQue corpus), but its average F1 across seven QA sets was 48.8, below the dense baseline's 57.0 under HippoRAG 2's setup. — [HippoRAG 2, Tables 2 and 12](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14802v2)
- In the RAG-vs-GraphRAG comparison RaptorRAG was the second-best multi-hop method (HotpotQA F1 61.31 vs RAG 60.04) and tied RAG on NovelQA (57.12). — [Han et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11371v3)
- In GraphRAG-Bench RAPTOR gave very faithful creative-generation answers (70.85 vs 49.21 for vanilla RAG on the novel corpus) at about 3.4k prompt tokens per query. — [GraphRAG-Bench (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05690)

### Inferences
- [inference-from-sources] Contextual retrieval for evidence cards can be done with no LLM calls: prepend a header built from the graph (document title, section, technique, application, scope label) to each card's text before BM25 and embedding. If the cards are written as self-contained claims (as a curated card should be), the main failure the technique fixes (orphaned chunks that say "the method" or "this fiber") is largely already removed. An LLM-written one-line context for each card is also trivially cheap (200 cards) if wanted.
- [inference-from-sources] RAPTOR's summary nodes would paraphrase, which is a liability for verbatim citation; a summary layer is better replaced by the graph's technique/application nodes, which already act as abstractions. RAPTOR becomes relevant only for long unstructured documents that have not been decomposed into cards.

### Gaps
- No independent replication of Anthropic's numbers found; the figures are vendor-reported on Anthropic's own mixed datasets and with an unnamed reranker not confirmed in the fetched text.
- RAPTOR cost figures on a small corpus not found beyond the MuSiQue table above.

## 4. Cache-augmented generation (CAG) / long-context "no retrieval" with prefix caching: accuracy, lost-in-the-middle, latency

### Takeaway
At 20-60k tokens the whole KB fits comfortably in a Qwen3.5-class window and published comparisons show long context matching or beating RAG at about 32k tokens, with RAG pulling ahead only at 128k, for hallucination-sensitive (unanswerable) questions, and for weaker models. The known costs are position effects and degradation on non-literal queries as length grows, so the strongest design is a compact, cache-friendly catalog in the prompt rather than full pages, with the question placed last.

### Cited Findings
- CAG preloads the whole knowledge base into the context and caches the KV state. On HotPotQA with Llama 3.1-8B, BERTScore: 21k-token set CAG 0.7951 vs best sparse RAG 0.7676 and best dense RAG 0.7582; 43k-token set 0.7821 vs 0.7633 / 0.7432; 85k-token set CAG 0.7407 vs sparse RAG 0.7535 (CAG loses). On SQuAD (21k, 32k, 50k tokens) CAG led in all three. Generation time on HotPotQA: CAG 0.85 s (21k) to 2.26 s (85k) vs dense RAG top-3 about 0.93-1.01 s and top-10 about 2.64-2.66 s; retrieval itself took about 0.38-0.49 s (dense). Limitations stated by the authors: one 8B model, two benchmarks, BERTScore, and the advantage narrows with size. Preprint. — [Chan et al., Don't Do RAG: CAG (Dec 2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.15605)
- Long-context (LC) vs RAG with three models (Gemini-1.5-Pro, GPT-4O, GPT-3.5-Turbo) on long-context datasets: LC averaged 49.70 / 48.67 / 32.07 against RAG 37.33 / 32.60 / 30.33. Their Self-Route (RAG first, fall back to LC when the model signals it cannot answer) answered 82% / 57% / 74% of queries via RAG and used about 38% / 61% / 39% of LC's tokens. The four RAG failure types were multi-step reasoning, general queries, long complex queries and implicit queries. — [Li et al., RAG or Long-Context LLMs? (2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16833)
- LaRA (2,326 cases; 11 LLMs; ICML 2025): average LC advantage of +2.4% over RAG at 32k and RAG advantage of +3.68% at 128k; LC wins on comparison tasks (+15.22 at 32k, +14.30 at 128k) and on reasoning with strong models; RAG wins hallucination detection, meaning questions the context cannot answer (-10.38 at 32k, -22.36 at 128k); weaker models benefit most from RAG; LC accuracy drops when the answer sits mid-context while RAG shows no clear position effect; structured documents such as papers favor LC. The authors' conclusion: no universal winner. Used hybrid embedding plus BM25 for RAG. — [LaRA (ICML 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09977v1)
- Lost in the Middle: accuracy on multi-document QA and key-value retrieval is highest when relevant information is at the start or end of the context and degrades significantly when it is in the middle, even for long-context models. Abstract-level; older models. — [Liu et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172)
- NoLiMa (ICML 2025): when needle and question share minimal lexical overlap, 11 of 13 long-context models fall below 50% of their short-context baseline at 32K tokens (GPT-4o 99.3% to 69.7%); reasoning/CoT only partly helps. — [NoLiMa (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05167)
- Chroma's context-rot report (18 LLMs, including Qwen3 and GPT-4.1/Claude 4/Gemini 2.5 families) found performance becomes less reliable as input length grows even on simple tasks, and that semantically similar distractors hurt more. Vendor technical report, not peer-reviewed; some of the blog coverage adds mechanistic claims that are not in the report. — [Chroma, Context Rot (July 2025)](https://trychroma.com/research/context-rot) via [secondary summary](https://wandb.ai/byyoung3/ml-news/reports/Chroma-Research-Warns-of-Context-Rot-as-LLMs-Falter-with-Long-Inputs--VmlldzoxMzczMjQ4MQ)
- vLLM Automatic Prefix Caching (APC) caches KV blocks of earlier queries so a shared prefix is not recomputed; the docs state it reduces only prefill time, not decoding, and works best when many queries share a long document (a manual, an annual report) or in multi-turn chats; it does not help when decoding dominates or when queries share no prefix; no benchmark numbers on the page. [vendor docs] — [vLLM APC docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/automatic_prefix_caching.html)
- Qwen3.5-397B-A17B is a hybrid architecture (15 blocks of three Gated DeltaNet-MoE layers followed by one Gated Attention-MoE layer), native context 262,144 tokens (extendable to about 1.01M with YaRN); the model card suggests keeping the context length at 128K or more to preserve thinking capability and does not mention prefix caching. — [Qwen3.5-397B-A17B model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-397B-A17B)
- vLLM prefix caching for Mamba/GDN hybrids is block-granular with large blocks: a vLLM 0.19.1 report on Qwen3.5-4B found a block size of 528 tokens, about 0% cache hit for a 479-token prompt, 95.4% at 552 tokens and 53.7% at 979 tokens (only full blocks are cached); a maintainer called it a limitation of the current hybrid-model prefix caching design, and the issue was marked stale in September 2026 with no fix visible. The vLLM docs describe an align mode where cache hits resume only at block boundaries. — [vLLM issue #40696](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/40696) and [vLLM APC docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/automatic_prefix_caching.html); LMCache notes the same alignment requirement and calls the mode experimental per a [secondary search summary](https://docs.lmcache.ai/recipes/qwen3_5.html)
- DeepSeek V4 Flash: several sources report a roughly 1M-token context and a KV cache about 7-10% of V3.2's through compressed sparse/heavily compressed attention (DeepSeek's own figures as relayed by NVIDIA and vLLM blogs); blogs describing a "V4.1 Flash" with different specs conflict and could not be verified. — [vLLM blog on DeepSeek V4](https://vllm.ai/blog/deepseek-v4) and [NVIDIA developer blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/?p=116127), via a [secondary search summary](https://mem0.ai/blog/deepseek-v4.1-flash-890-bytes-per-token-zero-bytes-of-persistent-memory)

### Inferences
- [inference-from-sources] For a 20-60k-token KB, the published evidence points to LC being on par or better than RAG at about 32k with strong models, especially for comparison questions ("NIR vs Raman for X"), and RAG being safer for unanswerable queries and long contexts. With 60k tokens you are between LaRA's 32k and 128k points, so test both rather than assume.
- [inference-from-sources] Mitigations that follow directly from the failure modes: (1) place the static KB first and the user question last (prefix-cache friendly, and the question sits at the high-attention end); (2) pass a compact catalog (card ID, claim, scope label, graph neighbors, about 10-20k tokens) rather than all verbatim quotes and page text, which shrinks the length-related risk; (3) require the model to return card IDs and have code attach the verbatim quote, so the citation does not depend on long-context fidelity; (4) include explicit "not covered" test queries because LC has the weakest abstention in LaRA.
- [inference-from-sources] Prefix caching: the savings are in prefill (TTFT) for the shared KB prefix, which for 20-60k tokens is the dominant latency term when the answer is short; for the Qwen3.5 hybrid, caching works at a large block granularity, so a 20-60k-token prefix loses at most about one block (hundreds of tokens), which is negligible, but it is documented as a design limitation and experimental on hybrids, so its availability and billing discount on your provider must be tested (cached-token counters or TTFT on repeated prefixes). If the provider does no prefix caching, each query pays full prefill of the catalog; at 30 requests/min and a few seconds of prefill per call this is workable but wasteful.
- [inference-from-sources] Anthropic's ">2x latency, up to 90% cost" numbers are for Claude with its own caching and should not be assumed for open-weight serving.

### Gaps
- No benchmark found for Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, DeepSeek V4 Flash or Gemma 4 31B on long-context-vs-RAG accuracy or lost-in-the-middle; all cited results use older models. Gemma 4 31B context length and caching behavior were not researched/found.
- No measured TTFT-with-prefix-cache numbers for 20-60k tokens on these models; vLLM docs give no figures. Whether the hosted API runs vLLM, enables APC and bills cached tokens differently is unknown.
- CAG paper's own results are small-scale (8B model, two QA sets); no independent replication found in this research.

## 5. Visual document retrieval for image-only slides: ColPali / ColQwen / VisRAG vs VLM transcription vs OCR

### Takeaway
Visual retrievers clearly beat classic OCR-plus-text pipelines on retrieval (ColPali 81.3 vs about 66 nDCG@5 on ViDoRe v1), but newer evidence shows that good parsed text plus a text reranker can match or beat them, and that VLM-based OCR is competitive with page-image RAG at the generation stage. For about 10-30 image-only slides inside a 60-page corpus where verbatim text quotes are required, VLM transcription with human verification is the pragmatic choice; ColPali-family indexes pay off only when the visual corpus is large.

### Cited Findings
- ColPali (page-image patches embedded with a VLM, late-interaction scoring) reaches 81.3 average nDCG@5 on ViDoRe, versus 65.5 (BM25 on Unstructured+OCR), 66.1 (BGE-M3 on OCR), 65.1 and 67.0 (BM25 / BGE-M3 on captioning). Indexing per page on an NVIDIA L4: 0.39 s for ColPali versus 7.22 s for the Unstructured pipeline with layout detection, OCR and captioning. Storage per page 257.5 KB (float16, 1,024 patches plus text tokens of 128 dims) versus 8.60 KB for BGE-M3; query latency about 30 ms vs about 22 ms for BGE-M3. Academic preprint; venue not verified here; benchmark and model by the same authors. — [ColPali (2024), full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01449v6)
- VisRAG (VLM-based RAG that embeds page images and feeds images to a VLM) claims a 20-40% end-to-end gain over a text-based RAG pipeline (abstract). — [VisRAG (Oct 2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10594)
- ViDoRe V3 (10 corpora, about 26,000 pages, 3,099 human-verified queries; ACL 2026 per listing): visual retriever ColEmbed-3B-v2 scores 59.8 nDCG@10 against 51.0 for the best textual model (Qwen3-8B) on NeMo-extracted Markdown; late-interaction models beat single-vector models; however, text pipeline plus a text reranker reached 63.6; a visual reranker added only +0.2. Hybrid top-5 visual plus top-5 text gave the best end-to-end result on hard queries (54.7% vs 54.5% best visual and 52.1% best text). Even strong VLMs localize evidence poorly: F1 0.09 (Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B) and 0.065 (Gemini 3 Pro) versus 0.60 human agreement. Chunking within pages or image descriptions did not help text retrieval. — [ViDoRe V3 (Jan 2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08620v1)
- OHRBench (ICCV 2025; 8,561 document images, 8,498 QA pairs): even the best OCR solution leaves at least a 14% end-to-end gap versus ground-truth text (F1 31.1 for Qwen2.5-VL-72B vs 36.1 ground truth; MinerU 30.0, Marker 29.5, InternVL2.5-78B 29.6); severe semantic (recognition-error) noise reduces RAG performance by nearly 50% for most retriever/LLM combinations; in a multimodal comparison with a Qwen2-VL-7B generator, VisRAG scored 40.2 versus 37.9 for MinerU OCR text and 40.6 for Qwen2.5-VL OCR text (ground truth 46.8), and VLMs and end-to-end models are weak on reading order. — [OHRBench (Dec 2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.02592)
- Parsing leaderboards: on OmniDocBench v1.5, PaddleOCR-VL-1.5 scores 94.50 overall, MinerU2.5 90.67 and Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B 89.15; on v1.6 PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 scores 96.33 and Qwen3-VL-235B 88.90. Scores differ across benchmark versions and the benchmark is described as noisy at the top; no slide-specific results found. [preprint and secondary leaderboards] — [PaddleOCR-VL-1.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03264) and [benchmarklist OmniDocBench 1.5](https://benchmarklist.com/benchmarks/omnidocbench_1_5/), via a [secondary search summary](https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/omnidocbench-is-saturated-what-s-next-for-ocr-benchmarks)

### Inferences
- [inference-from-sources] Retrieval over about 60 pages does not need a visual retriever: a page-level or card-level text index built from transcriptions is enough, and the extra storage and multi-vector serving of ColPali-class models (about 258 KB/page) buy nothing at this size. Their value appears when thousands of pages (many with charts and tables) must be indexed without paying for per-page VLM transcription.
- [inference-from-sources] The real risk for image-only slides is silent transcription error (wavelength, concentration, units, RMSEP values). OHRBench's finding that recognition errors cut RAG performance severely supports a verification step: have two different VLMs transcribe each slide and diff the outputs; have a human check the specific quotes that become cards (70-200 cards is a small review job); keep a crop of the source region as a citation proof. The transcribed text is then the "verbatim" source of record and should be labeled as VLM-transcribed.
- [inference-from-sources] ColPali-class models (or VLM question answering directly on the slide image) are still useful as an offline QA check: ask what a slide says in image space and compare with the transcription.

### Gaps
- No slide-specific benchmark (vendor slides with spectra, schematics) found; none for spectroscopy figures or tables.
- ColQwen2/2.5 numbers were not obtained in this research (only ColPali v1 and ViDoRe V3 aggregate). VisRAG's own tables beyond the abstract were not read.
- Cost of VLM transcription with the specific models in this deployment (Qwen3.5 is natively multimodal per the model card, "causal language model with a vision encoder") was not measured; at 30 requests/min and tens of image pages this is a minutes-scale task.

## 6. Citation precision: quote spans vs chunks; late chunking

### Takeaway
No benchmark was found that measures verbatim-span fidelity directly. Established citation metrics (ALCE) judge entailment rather than exact quotes, and sentence-level pruning/reranking (Provence) exists but is not tuned for exact attribution. Late chunking is a cheap boost for long documents but needs token-level embeddings that an OpenAI-compatible embeddings endpoint typically does not expose. For evidence cards, the unit of retrieval is already a span, so enforcement should be programmatic.

### Cited Findings
- ALCE scores citation recall (do the cited documents entail each sentence) and citation precision (is each citation needed), using a T5-11B NLI model; human agreement was 85.1% for recall and 77.6% for precision, the NLI model cannot detect partial support and yields false positives on irrelevant citations; the original paper reported that even the best models lacked full citation support about half the time on ELI5. ALCE includes an extracted-snippet option. Widely used benchmark (2023; venue not verified here), figures as relayed by a search summary. — [ALCE: Enabling LLMs to Generate Text with Citations](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.14627) and [summary of metric reliability](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.14627)
- Provence treats context pruning as token/sentence-level sequence labeling conditioned on the query and merges it with reranking, reporting negligible performance drop and near-zero added cost, with a released model; the full benchmark numbers were not verified. — [Provence (Jan 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16214v1)
- Late chunking (embed the whole document with a long-context model, then mean-pool token embeddings per chunk) improved nDCG@10 versus naive chunking by +1.90 (SciFact, 64.20 to 66.10), +1.34 (TRECCOVID), +0.59 (FiQA), +6.52 (NFCorpus, 23.46 to 29.98), and 0 on short-document Quora; embedding the unchunked document was even better on TRECCOVID and NFCorpus. Needs a long-context embedding model (8,192 tokens in this study, jina-embeddings-v2-small-en), small single-model evaluation; Jina vendor blog. — [Jina AI, Late Chunking (2024)](https://jina.ai/news/late-chunking-in-long-context-embedding-models/)
- Visual-evidence localization is a known weak point: VLMs reach F1 0.09 vs human 0.60 for evidence localization on ViDoRe V3 pages. — [ViDoRe V3](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08620v1)
- GraphRAG variants were more faithful but covered less evidence than vanilla RAG in creative-generation tasks (faithfulness higher, evidence coverage usually lower). — [GraphRAG-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05690)

### Inferences
- [inference-from-sources] With cards, you already have sentence-level evidence units; chunking and late chunking are not the problem. If you ever embed whole pages or papers, late chunking requires token-level outputs from the embedding model, which a standard /v1/embeddings call (pooled vector per input) does not return; contextual headers are the API-compatible alternative.
- [inference-from-sources] Citation precision should be enforced in code rather than hoped for from the model: (1) the model answers only with card IDs (or must paste the quote); (2) post-hoc string check (normalized whitespace and hyphenation) that every quoted span occurs on the cited page of the source text; (3) drop or flag any claim whose cited card ID is not in the retrieved/catalog set; (4) a separate entailment check (a small LLM or NLI pass) that the cited quote supports the sentence. This turns "verbatim by construction" into a measurable guarantee, and ALCE-style recall/precision on a gold set of enquiries gives the headline metric. These checks add one or two LLM calls per answer, which matters at 30 requests/min.
- [inference-from-sources] A sentence-level pruner (Provence-like) is not needed at 200 cards; the card already is the minimal quote.

### Gaps
- No benchmark of exact-quote or span-level attribution for RAG found; ALCE/NLI is the closest and is documented as noisy.
- Whether Qwen3-Embedding-4B exposes token-level outputs through the deployed API, and its maximum input length, were not checked.
- Provence results were taken from an abstract-level summary; not verified against tables.

## 7. Recommended design for a tiny, high-precision corpus, and what changes at 10x / 100x

### Takeaway
Keep the hand-built typed graph plus evidence cards as the source of truth, put a compact cached card catalog into the prompt (CAG-style) for selection, and have code attach verbatim quotes and verify them; keep the existing graph-first plus BM25 plus dense RRF path as the fallback and as a measured baseline. Add a reranker, contextual headers, and then LLM-built graph methods only when a gold-set evaluation shows the specific failure each one fixes, which the literature says arrives roughly at 1,000+ units for rerankers/contextual retrieval and beyond the context window for CAG.

### Cited Findings
- Long-context beats RAG on average at about 32k tokens with strong models, and RAG wins at 128k, on unanswerable-question detection, and for weaker models. — [LaRA](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09977v1)
- Plain RAG matches or beats GraphRAG on fact retrieval; graphs help on complex reasoning, and LLM-extracted graphs miss about a third of answer entities and abstain poorly. — [GraphRAG-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05690); [Han et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11371v3)
- Contextual retrieval plus reranking cut retrieval failure by 67% on large chunked corpora, and the same source says to skip RAG entirely under about 200k tokens. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)
- A graph without attached source text fell to 15-20% accuracy while the same graph with attached chunks matched GraphRAG at 90% (single 20-question study). — [arXiv 2511.05991](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.05991v1)
- Visual retrievers lead on page-image corpora, but text pipelines with a good reranker can match them, and parsing errors cost RAG 14% to about 50% depending on severity. — [ViDoRe V3](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08620v1); [OHRBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.02592)
- Prefix caching speeds up only prefill of a repeated prefix; hybrid-attention models (Qwen3.5) cache at a large block granularity. — [vLLM APC docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/automatic_prefix_caching.html); [vLLM issue #40696](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/40696)

### Inferences
Recommended design at the current size (about 60 pages, 70-200 cards, [inference-from-sources] throughout):
1. Source of truth: cards (claim, verbatim quote, page, scope label) plus typed graph; every card gets a deterministic contextual header from the graph (document, section, technique, application, scope) used for BM25 and embedding text. Cost: none.
2. Selection: graph-first scope filtering, then RRF over BM25 and Qwen3-Embedding-4B on the headered cards, returning generously (top 20-30 cards, which at card length is a few thousand tokens). Optionally also place the full compact catalog (about 10-20k tokens) as a stable cached prefix and let the model choose IDs (CAG-style); decide between "retrieved top-k only" and "catalog in context" by evaluation, since LaRA shows LC is stronger on comparison questions and RAG on abstention.
3. Answering: the model returns claims with card IDs; code injects the verbatim quote and page and runs string and entailment verification; no free-form quotes; explicit "not in corpus" path with tests.
4. Image-only slides: VLM transcription by two models, diff, human spot-check of quoted spans, source-crop retained; no ColPali.
5. Evaluation harness first: 50-100 real enquiries with gold card IDs, expected "not covered" cases, and metrics recall@k, citation precision and recall (ALCE-style), quote-match rate, refusal accuracy, p50 latency. This is also the only way to decide each upgrade below, because no source evaluates these methods on a corpus this small.
6. Rate budget: at 30 requests/min the best design uses 1-3 LLM calls per answer (answer, optional verifier); avoid multi-call query rewriting or LLM listwise reranking.

When each option becomes worthwhile (thresholds are judgment calls, anchored on the cited context-window and corpus-size results):

| Option | Today (~200 cards, 20-60k tokens) | About 10x (2k cards, 200-600k tokens) | About 100x (20k cards, millions of tokens) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dense-only vs hybrid RRF | Hybrid (already done) | Hybrid | Hybrid, tuned weights |
| Cross-encoder reranker | Test only; likely marginal | Add (bge-reranker-v2-m3 for latency, Qwen3-Reranker-4B for quality); rerank 50-100 to 10-20 | Standard |
| Contextual headers / contextual retrieval | Free deterministic headers | Add LLM-written context only if cards lose self-containment | Standard (Anthropic-style) |
| CAG / full catalog in context | Yes, catalog form | Does not fit economically beyond the 262k native window and LaRA shows RAG ahead at 128k; keep only a titles-level map in context | No |
| LLM-built graph (HippoRAG 2 class) | No: graph already hand-built, no accuracy evidence at this size | Trial only if multi-hop evals fail; HippoRAG 2 has the best accuracy-per-token profile among graph methods | Reasonable, with extraction audit |
| GraphRAG (community summaries) / LightRAG | No | Only for "themes across the corpus" questions | Only for corpus-wide sense-making; watch abstention |
| RAPTOR | No | Only for long unstructured documents not decomposed into cards | Same |
| ColPali / ColQwen / VisRAG | No | Only if many image-heavy PDFs make per-page VLM transcription too slow | Yes for large image-heavy sets, as a second retriever alongside text (ViDoRe V3 hybrid best) |
| Late chunking | No (cards are the units) | Only if embedding long unsplit documents with a model that exposes token embeddings | Same |

- [inference-from-sources] The hand-built graph does not scale linearly in curation effort; at 10x, LLM-assisted card and graph extraction with human review becomes the bottleneck, and the Han et al. coverage figure (about 65% of answer entities) is the warning that LLM extraction alone is not enough.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head study of hand-curated typed graph vs LLM graph RAG on a corpus under 1,000 units; the recommendation to keep the hand-built graph rests on indirect evidence (coverage, cost, graph-without-text failure) and on the mismatch between benchmark tasks and entity-scoped enquiries.
- Thresholds in the table are inference, not measured crossover points; the crossover between CAG and RAG for Qwen3.5-class models was not found in the literature.
- Cost and latency of the actual deployment (provider, whether prefix caching is on, reranker endpoint, token pricing) are unknown and decide several rows.
