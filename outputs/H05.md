## Recommendation

For samples of incompletely known composition, testing several optical methods supports selection of the most suitable method or combination.[^S01-p03-unknown-composition] Compare MIR, NIR and Raman on representative retained good and off-specification samples before choosing a method.
  - Conditions: Define the measurement target before selecting a method or sensor configuration.

## Spectral range
- MIR: 4000–400 cm⁻¹ (2.5–25 µm); fiber: Polycrystalline (AgHal) IR fiber, Hollow glass waveguide. The nominal MIR range is 4000–400 cm⁻¹ (2.5–25 µm).[^S01-p09-mir-range] The usable range depends on the instrument, fiber and probe and is specified for the selected system.
- NIR: 780–2500 nm; fiber: VIS-NIR silica fiber. The nominal NIR range is 780–2500 nm.[^S01-p10-nir-range] The usable range depends on the instrument, fiber and probe and is specified for the selected system. NIR bands overlap, so quantitative interpretation may suggest chemometric calibration.[^S01-p10-nir-chemometrics]
- Raman: Raman shift in cm⁻¹; detection region 400–1100 nm; fiber: UV-VIS silica fiber, VIS-NIR silica fiber. Raman detection happens in the 400–1100 nm visible/short-wave NIR region.[^S01-p11-raman-region] Raman spectra are reported as Raman shift in cm⁻¹, separate from the excitation wavelength.[^S01-p11-raman-shift] The usable range depends on the instrument, fiber and probe and is specified for the selected system. State the excitation wavelength separately from the Raman shift and choose it with the sample's fluorescence and the instrument in mind. Fluorescence background can swamp the Raman signal of many organic compounds.[^S01-p11-raman-fluorescence]

- **Validation:** Test any screening-derived relationship on independent material excluded from method selection and calibration.
