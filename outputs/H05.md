## Recommendation

Testing several optical methods supports method selection when sample composition is incompletely known.[^S01-p03-unknown-composition] As the first experiment, compare MIR, NIR and Raman on retained representative good and off-specification samples before choosing a method.

## Spectral range
- MIR: 4000–400 cm⁻¹ (2.5–25 µm); fiber: Polycrystalline (AgHal) IR fiber, Hollow glass waveguide. The nominal MIR range is 4000–400 cm⁻¹ (2.5–25 µm).[^S01-p09-mir-range] The usable range depends on the instrument, fiber and probe and is specified for the selected system. Strong MIR absorption requires very thin samples or ATR sampling.[^S01-p05-mir-thin-atr]
- NIR: 780–2500 nm; fiber: VIS-NIR silica fiber. The nominal NIR range is 780–2500 nm.[^S01-p10-nir-range] The usable range depends on the instrument, fiber and probe and is specified for the selected system. The broad absorption bands observed in this region overlap, suggesting the use of chemometrics for quantitative and qualitative analysis of spectral data.[^S01-p10-nir-chemometrics]
- Raman: Raman shift in cm⁻¹; detection region 400–1100 nm; fiber: UV-VIS silica fiber, VIS-NIR silica fiber. Raman detection happens in the 400–1100 nm visible/short-wave NIR region.[^S01-p11-raman-region] Raman spectra are reported as Raman shift in cm⁻¹, separate from the excitation wavelength.[^S01-p11-raman-shift] The usable range depends on the instrument, fiber and probe and is specified for the selected system. State the excitation wavelength separately from the Raman shift and choose it with the sample's fluorescence and the instrument in mind. Fluorescence background can swamp the Raman signal of many organic compounds.[^S01-p11-raman-fluorescence]

## First feasibility experiment
**Objective:** Test whether spectral differences between good and off-specification samples can be linked to a defined quality variable.

1. Clarify appearance, application, intended use, references, criteria and target range.
2. Collect representative retained good and off-specification samples.
3. Establish sample status and independent target reference values.
4. Record and compare spectra under documented conditions.
5. Validate on independent material against pre-agreed criteria.
6. Proceed or stop according to validation and target definition.

- **Samples:** Collect representative retained good and off-specification samples covering the relevant process states.
- **References:** Link each sample to its documented specification status and, once the target is defined, independent reference values.
- **Set-up:** Record MIR, NIR and Raman spectra from the same retained samples under documented measurement conditions.
- **Comparison:** Compare the screening methods against documented sample status and any established target reference values.
- **Validation:** Validate any resulting relationship to the defined target on independent material excluded from screening and model development.
- **Go / no-go:** Defer method and sensor selection unless a target is defined and independent validation meets criteria agreed with you before testing.
- **Not demonstrated:** Do not treat screening separation alone as proof of contaminant identity or in-line performance.

## Decision basis
Base any later method and sensor selection on comparison against independent reference values on independent material, using criteria agreed with you before testing.
