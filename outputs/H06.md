## Recommendation
- **MIR + NIR + Raman · multi-method screening on retained samples** — SCREENING, evidence Moderate. The LED NIR study provides a proof-of-concept demonstration of the LED-based sensor for tumor discrimination.[^S03-p14-proof-of-concept] Compare MIR-ATR, NIR surface measurements and Raman probes on retained samples before selecting a method.
  - Conditions: Make any research-stage method selection conditional on comparison with reference results against agreed acceptance criteria.
  - Limitations: Spectra of frozen/thawed biopsies can differ from in-vivo measurements.[^S03-p03-ex-vivo-caveat] The study identifies false negatives as unacceptable in oncological diagnostics.[^S03-p14-false-negatives]

## Spectral range
- MIR: 4000–400 cm⁻¹ (2.5–25 µm); fiber: Polycrystalline (AgHal) IR fiber, Hollow glass waveguide. The nominal MIR range is 4000–400 cm⁻¹ (2.5–25 µm).[^S01-p09-mir-range] The usable range depends on the instrument, fiber and probe and is specified for the selected system.
- NIR: 780–2500 nm; fiber: VIS-NIR silica fiber. The nominal NIR range is 780–2500 nm.[^S01-p10-nir-range] The usable range depends on the instrument, fiber and probe and is specified for the selected system. NIR absorption bands overlap, supporting chemometric analysis for qualitative and quantitative interpretation.[^S01-p10-nir-chemometrics]
- Raman: Raman shift in cm⁻¹; detection region 400–1100 nm; fiber: UV-VIS silica fiber, VIS-NIR silica fiber. Raman detection happens in the 400–1100 nm visible/short-wave NIR region.[^S01-p11-raman-region] Raman spectra are reported as Raman shift in cm⁻¹, separate from the excitation wavelength.[^S01-p11-raman-shift] The usable range depends on the instrument, fiber and probe and is specified for the selected system. State the excitation wavelength separately from the Raman shift and choose it with the sample's fluorescence and the instrument in mind. Fluorescence background can overwhelm Raman signals from many organic compounds.[^S01-p11-raman-fluorescence]

## Risks and uncertainty
- The relationship between retained-sample spectra and live-surgery boundary decisions is unknown. _(unknown)_ Effect: Unsupported transfer to clinical decisions. Mitigation: Keep feasibility testing research-only.
- NIR reflection is sensitive to surface characteristics.[^S01-p17-reflection-surface] Effect: Surface variation may confound spectral comparisons. Mitigation: Assess surface effects during retained-sample screening.

## First feasibility experiment
**Objective:** Assess research-stage feasibility for tumor-boundary discrimination on retained tissue samples.

1. Clarify requirements, reference method and acceptance criteria.
2. Select representative retained tissue samples and document their states.
3. Obtain matched reference assessments.
4. Set up and compare MIR, NIR and Raman measurements.
5. Validate on independent retained samples.
6. Decide whether results justify further research under agreed criteria.

- **Samples:** Select retained tissue samples spanning the client-agreed target range and document their preparation and storage states.
- **References:** Obtain matched comparison values using a reference method agreed with the client.
- **Set-up:** Acquire MIR, NIR and Raman spectra under documented conditions, recording Raman excitation separately from Raman shift.
- **Comparison:** Compare each method with the reference assessments against client-agreed criteria set before testing, explicitly accounting for false-negative outcomes.
- **Validation:** Test each fixed analysis on independent retained samples excluded from its development.
- **Go / no-go:** Use the agreed criteria to decide whether further research is warranted, not whether to authorize surgical use.
- **Not demonstrated:** Clinical performance of the proposed setup, including its false-negative behavior in vivo, remains unknown. _(unknown)_

## Decision basis
Base research continuation on comparison with reference assessments on independent retained material against criteria agreed with the client before testing.
